\section{Method}

\subsection{Face extraction}
From each video we take 32 evenly spaced frames. Faces are detected with the S$^3$FD detector
and 68 landmarks are estimated with FAN, using the same detector for every dataset so that
preprocessing never differs between training and test data. When a frame contains several faces
we follow the face that overlaps the previous frame's face, falling back to the largest one. The
crop is a square of 1.3 times the face box, resized to $224\times224$. Videos without a usable
face are logged with a reason rather than silently dropped; \res{N} of \res{N} videos were
skipped across all datasets.

\subsection{Model}
The backbone is a ViT-B/16~\citep{dosovitskiy2021vit} initialized from ImageNet-21k
weights. A linear head on the CLS token produces one logit per crop. We insert LoRA
adapters~\citep{hu2022lora} of rank $r=16$ and scale $\alpha=32$ into the query, key and value
projections of every attention layer and train only the adapters and the head: about 0.6M of
86M parameters. Input normalization is part of the model so that training, testing and the
released inference code see identical inputs.

\subsection{Training data}
\paragraph{Self-blended images.} Following~\citet{shiohara2022sbi}, with probability 0.5 a real
training crop is turned into a synthetic fake. A copy of the face is altered (a per-channel
color shift, a contrast change, sharpening or blurring, and a small resize and translation) and
blended back over the original through the convex hull of its landmarks. The mask is randomly
grown or shrunk, softened with a Gaussian blur, and scaled by a blend ratio drawn from
$\{0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1\}$. The result carries seams and color and frequency mismatches without
the style of any particular generator.

\paragraph{Augmentation.} After blending, every crop goes through horizontal flips, JPEG
recompression (quality 30 to 100), Gaussian blur or downscaling, Gaussian noise, color jitter and
cutout. Blending first means the synthetic artifacts are degraded the same way real fakes are.

\paragraph{Balancing.} FF++ has four fakes per real video, so each epoch samples real and fake
crops with equal probability.

\subsection{Optimization}
We use AdamW (learning rate $10^{-4}$, weight decay 0.05), a cosine schedule with one epoch of
linear warmup, bf16 mixed precision, batch size 16 with gradient accumulation to an effective
batch of 64, and gradient clipping at 1.0, for 20 epochs. The checkpoint with the best FF++
validation video AUC is kept. The test sets are never used to choose a model, a threshold or
when to stop. Training peaks at 1.3\,GB of GPU memory and runs at about 185 images per second on
an RTX~4060 Laptop GPU.

\subsection{From frames to calibrated bands}
A video's score is the mean of its frame probabilities. One temperature $T$ is fitted on FF++
validation frames by minimizing log loss~\citep{guo2017calibration}, and every frame logit is
divided by $T$ before averaging. The output is then mapped to three bands. The lower cut-off
leaves at most 5\% of validation fakes in \emph{likely real}, and the upper cut-off leaves at
most 5\% of validation reals in \emph{likely manipulated}; everything between is
\emph{uncertain}. The uncertain band is never narrower than 0.2, because FF++ validation
separates more cleanly than unseen data and would otherwise squeeze it away. Every output
carries a note that the score is an estimate, not proof.
