\section{Introduction}

Realistic face manipulation is now cheap. Face swapping, reenactment and fully generated faces
can be produced with open-source tools and consumer hardware, and manipulated videos of real
people are used for fraud, harassment and disinformation. Automatic detection is one part of the
response, but only if detectors work on the videos they will actually meet.

That is where current detectors fall short. Trained and tested on the same benchmark, such as
FaceForensics++ (FF++)~\citep{rossler2019faceforensics}, many models score close to 0.99 AUC.
Tested on a different dataset, such as Celeb-DF~\citep{li2020celebdf}, the same models often fall
to between 0.65 and 0.75~\citep{li2020celebdf,yan2023deepfakebench}. In practice the method used
to make a fake is almost never one the detector was trained on, so cross-dataset performance,
not same-dataset performance, is the number that matters.

A common explanation is that detectors learn shortcuts specific to the training generators,
such as a particular color mismatch or compression trace, instead of the more general marks that
manipulation leaves behind. Two lines of work address this. One synthesizes training fakes that
only contain generic blending artifacts~\citep{li2020facexray,shiohara2022sbi}. The other starts
from large pretrained vision models whose features generalize well and adapts them
lightly~\citep{ojha2023towards}.

We combine both in a model small enough to train on a single 8\,GB laptop GPU, and we focus on
measuring it honestly. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A ViT-B/16 detector fine-tuned with LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} on FF++ face crops, trained
        with self-blended images~\citep{shiohara2022sbi} and degradation augmentation.
  \item A full cross-dataset evaluation on Celeb-DF v2, DFDC, DeeperForensics-1.0 and
        WildDeepfake, plus a leave-one-method-out protocol on FF++, against Xception,
        EfficientNet-B4 and a plainly fine-tuned ViT trained with the same pipeline, over three
        seeds with bootstrap confidence intervals.
  \item Calibrated outputs reported as three bands (likely real, uncertain, likely manipulated)
        rather than a binary verdict, with calibration error measured on every test set.
  \item Robustness, fairness and failure analyses, and ablations of every design choice,
        explaining why the chosen settings help.
\end{itemize}
